Headline: The Hidden Structure of an Exactly Solvable Quantum Spin Liquid

We have derived the exact physical density matrix—the mathematical object that fully describes a quantum state—of the Kitaev quantum spin liquid in terms of its original spins. Remarkably, although the Kitaev model has been exactly solvable for two decades and its quantum entanglement has been extensively studied, this fundamental description of its physical quantum state had remained unavailable. Our result reveals how the density matrix is organized by the fractionalized particles and emergent gauge structure that characterize a quantum spin liquid, with this organization governed in part by an unusual type of symmetry known as a one-form symmetry. This organization provides a microscopic explanation for the known separation of the model’s entanglement into matter and gauge contributions. It also uncovers a previously hidden effect: subsystems containing an odd number of spins behave differently from those containing an even number, reflecting the fermionic nature of the fractionalized matter. Because density matrices can describe quantum states beyond zero temperature and in the presence of an environment, our formulation provides a starting point for asking how the defining quantum structures of a spin liquid survive under more realistic conditions and how they can be characterized using tools from quantum information.

\section{Projective Representation of Configuration Algebra}\label{appendix:projective-rep}

In this appendix, we discuss the fundamental properties of the phase factor $\varphi(\mathfrak{D},\mathfrak{D}')$ defined in Eq.~\eqref{equ.Sigma-representation} that emerges from the projective representation generated by string operators introduced in Sec.~\ref{sec:density-matrix-whole}. The purpose is to directly demonstrate that the algebra of the spin representation, as given by Eq.~\eqref{equ.U-rep-algebra}, is equivalent to that of the Majorana representation, as given by Eq.~\eqref{equ.V-rep-algebra}.

Each joint, marked by dashed circles in the figure, contributes a factor $\pm i$ due to the algebra of Pauli spin operators. In contrast, both endpoints of the string $\mathfrak{D}_{\bo{Od}}$ are in the same sublattice, and there are an odd number (three, in this example) of joints on the string. A loop, located in the right portion of the figure, doesn't have endpoints, and there are always an even number (twelve in this example) of joints on a closed loop.

\end{figure}

We begin with the most basic property of the string operator: it squares to one up to a sign. Let $\mathsf{S}$ be an element of the projective Pauli group $\mathcal{P}_N \equiv \lrg{\mathds{1}, \sigma^x, \sigma^y, \sigma^z}^{\otimes N}$ of size $N$, which is the traditional Pauli group without phases $\lrg{\pm 1, \pm i}$. We define $\mathsf{S}_{\mathfrak{D}} \in \mathcal{P}_N$ as an operator that is proportional to the string operator of the edge configuration $\mathfrak{D}$, i.e., $\mathsf{S}_\mathfrak{D} = \omega_\mathfrak{D} \Sigma_\mathfrak{D}$ with a phase $\omega_\mathfrak{D}\in\lrg{\pm1, \pm i}$ arising from the algebra of Pauli spin operators. According to the definition of the string operator given by Eq.~\eqref{equ.string-operator-def}
and the structure of strings illustrated in \autoref{fig:joints}, each joint on the string contributes a $\pm i$ to the phase factor $\omega_\mathfrak{D}$ due to the algebra $\sigma^\mu\sigma^\nu = i\epsilon^{\mu\nu\lambda}\sigma^\lambda$. Owing to this, the following relation holds for $\omega_\mathfrak{D}$,
\[
\omega_\mathfrak{D}^2 = \lrm{(\pm i)^{2}}^{\text{\# of joints on $\mathfrak{D}$}} = (-1)^{\text{\# of joints on $\mathfrak{D}$}}.
\]
Combining this result and the fact that every element in $\mathcal{P}_N$ squares to one
\[
\mathsf{S}^2 = \omega_\mathfrak{D}^2\Sigma_\mathfrak{D}^2  = \mathds{1},
\]
we conclude that 
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sigma-joints}
    \Sigma_\mathfrak{D}^2 = (-1)^{\text{\# of joints on $\mathfrak{D}$}} \mathds{1}.
\end{equation}
This relation is equivalent to the Majorana representation. For example, a dimer as a string has no joints, and its Majorana representation is $\mathcal{M} = ic_ic_j$, which squares to one $(ic_ic_j)^2 = 1$, which satisfies the relation $(-1)^{\text{\# of joints}} = 1$. In contrast, a string consisting of two connected dimers has one joint, and the Majorana representation is $(ic_1c_2)(ic_3c_2)$. It is straightforward to show this operator squares to minus one due to fermionic anti-commutation. 

\begin{equation}\label{eq:phi-self-with-vacuum}
    \varphi(\varnothing, \mathfrak{D}) = \varphi(\mathfrak{D}, \varnothing) = 1
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}\label{eq:phi-self-with-vacuum}
    \varphi(\mathfrak{D},\mathfrak{D}) = 
    \left\{
    \begin{array}{ccc}
        +1 &; &  \\
        +1 &; &  \\
        -1 &; & \text{otherwise}
    \end{array}
    \right.
\end{equation}
